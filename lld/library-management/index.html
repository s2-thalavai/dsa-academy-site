Library management

A library system is about rules that interact: loan limits, due dates and fines, and a reservation queue that holds a returned copy for the next person in line. Keep each rule in one place and the design stays simple.

Low-level design

Picture it. The front desk has a card for every member, a slip for every loan, and a waiting list clipped to each title. When a book comes back, the clerk checks the waiting list before putting it on the shelf.

Requirements

Functional

  • Catalogue books (one title, many physical copies) and members.
  • Borrow: at most 2 loans per member, 14 days each; refuse while fines are unpaid.
  • Return: charge 0.25 per day late; if someone reserved the title, hold the copy for them and notify them.
  • Reserve a title when no copy is on the shelf; search titles.

Non-functional

  • Rules (limits, loan period, fine rate) are easy to change.
  • Notifications are decoupled from the library logic (Observer).

Entities and relationships

  • Book vs copy: a book is a title (ISBN); a copy is a physical item with a barcode. Loans and holds are about copies; reservations and search are about books.
  • Where each copy is: on the shelf (in shelf[isbn]), on loan (in loans), or on the hold shelf for one member (in heldFor). Exactly one of those at a time.
  • Rules as constants: MAX_LOANS, LOAN_DAYS, FINE_PER_DAY. A member-type policy (students vs staff) would turn them into a Strategy.

Design patterns used

  • Facade: Library offers the use cases; callers never touch the maps.
  • Observer: listeners are told when a held copy is ready, whether by email, SMS or an in-app message.
  • Queue per title: reservations are first come, first served.

How a request flows

Cost of each operation

OperationTimeWhy
borrow / giveBackO(1)Hash lookups plus a queue operation.
reserveO(1)Append to the title's waitlist.
searchO(titles)A substring scan; an inverted index makes it fast at scale.

Code

Days are passed in, so the scenario is exact. Notifications are collected and printed under the action that caused them.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a book and its copies as one object, so two people can't borrow two copies.
  • Putting a returned copy back on the shelf before checking reservations.
  • Computing fines from wall-clock time inside the method instead of taking the day as input (untestable).
  • Spreading the loan-limit check across controllers instead of one place in borrow.

Interview follow-ups

  • Expire holds that aren't collected within 3 days and move to the next person.
  • Different limits and loan periods for different member types.
  • Renewals, refused when someone is waiting.
  • Several branches with transfers between them.
