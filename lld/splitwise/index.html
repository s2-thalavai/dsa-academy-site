Splitwise

Shared expenses: someone pays, the cost is split equally, by exact amounts or by percentages, and the app tracks who owes whom. Then it simplifies the debts so the group settles with as few payments as possible.

Low-level design

Picture it. After a trip, everyone puts their receipts on the table. Rather than everyone paying everyone back, you work out each person's overall position, and the people who are down simply pay the people who are up.

Requirements

Functional

  • Add an expense: payer, total, participants and a split type (equal, exact, percent).
  • Validate splits: exact amounts must add up to the total; percentages to 100.
  • Show pairwise balances (who owes whom, netted in both directions).
  • Suggest a short list of payments that settles everyone.

Non-functional

  • Money is exact: integer cents, with the leftover cent of an equal split assigned deterministically.
  • New split types without editing the expense code (Open/closed).

Entities and relationships

  • Shares: each strategy returns person → cents and must add up to the total exactly. Equal split of 100.00 three ways is 33.34 + 33.33 + 33.33. Percent split rounds down and gives the leftover cents to the first people.
  • Ledger: for each share of someone other than the payer, add a debt from them to the payer, netting against any debt in the other direction, so there's at most one direction per pair.
  • Simplify: compute each person's net position (owed minus owing). Then repeatedly let the biggest debtor pay the biggest creditor as much as possible. This needs at most n − 1 payments. (Finding the true minimum is NP-hard; it's LeetCode 465.)

Design patterns used

  • Strategy: one class per split type, chosen per expense.
  • Facade: ExpenseManager hides the ledger and the simplification behind a few methods.

How a request flows

Cost of each operation

OperationTimeWhy
addExpenseO(p)One share and one ledger update per participant.
balancesO(pairs)Walk the ledger.
simplifyO(n²)Up to n − 1 payments, each finding the largest debtor and creditor; a heap makes it O(n log n).

Code

Three expenses between four friends, a rejected split, the pairwise balances, then the payments that settle everything.

Common mistakes

  • Floating-point money: 0.1 + 0.2 is not 0.3. Use integer cents.
  • Losing the leftover cent in equal splits, so balances don't add up to zero.
  • Keeping debts in both directions between a pair instead of netting them.
  • Updating balances before validating the split.

Interview follow-ups

  • Groups, with balances per group and overall.
  • Multiple currencies with exchange rates at the time of the expense.
  • Edit and delete expenses (store expenses, recompute or apply the inverse).
  • Minimise the number of payments exactly for small groups (backtracking).
