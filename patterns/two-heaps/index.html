Two heaps

Split the data into a smaller half and a larger half: a max-heap for the smaller half and a min-heap for the larger half. Their tops sit right at the middle, so the median is always one or two lookups away.

Coding pattern

Picture it. Lining people up by height and drawing a line through the middle. You only ever need to know the tallest person on the short side and the shortest person on the tall side. When someone new arrives, they join one side, and if the sides get unbalanced, the person nearest the line steps across.

Recognise it

  • A running median of a stream.
  • The median of a sliding window.
  • Repeatedly choosing the best item from one pool while items move between pools (IPO: affordable projects vs the most profitable).

The template

low  = max-heap   (smaller half)
high = min-heap   (larger half)
add(x):
    push x into low; move low's top into high          ← keeps every low ≤ every high
    if high has more items than low: move high's top into low
median:
    if sizes are equal: (low.top + high.top) / 2
    else: low.top

How it works

The invariant has two parts: every value in low is at most every value in high, and low has the same number of items as high or one more. Pushing through low into high and then rebalancing keeps both true with at most three heap operations per insert.

Because both tops are reachable in O(1), the median query is O(1) and insert is O(log n). Sorting the stream after every insert would be O(n log n) per insert.

Cost of each operation

OperationTimeWhy
Add a numberO(log n)At most three heap pushes or pops.
Get the medianO(1)Read one or both tops.
Sliding window medianO(n log k)Needs deletion of the item leaving the window, usually lazily.

Code

Medians are printed with one decimal place in every language so the outputs match.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting that Java's and Python's heaps are min-heaps; store negatives (Python) or pass a reverse comparator (Java) for the max-heap.
  • Letting the sizes differ by more than one.
  • Integer division when averaging two middle values; the median of 1 and 2 is 1.5.
  • Overflow when adding two large ints before dividing; add as long or double.
